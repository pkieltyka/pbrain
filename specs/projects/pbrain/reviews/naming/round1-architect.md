# Naming, round 1: @architect

Written before reading the other papers.

**Keep `Item`.** An item is one piece of knowledge with its header, in one Markdown file: a decision of two sentences, a learning, an observation, a note, or a source. Only the `source` kind is a document in the everyday sense, and for a linked item even that document stays at its origin and pbrain holds a pointer and a summary. The spec already uses "document" for something else, the outside material an item is made from: the skill tells an agent "from a document, record what is reusable as items", and spec 17 separates a task "that needs a learning" from one "that needs the document". Calling the unit `Document` would erase the distinction that sentence teaches, and would make a five-line decision a "document". It would also collide in the code, where `render` has a `Doc`, the budgeted output document. The alternatives are no better: `Note` is one of the five kinds, `Memory` suggests an agent's private store when half the point is decisions made by people, and `Entry` and `Record` are as neutral as `Item` without being clearer. The cost of a rename is real and buys nothing: "item" appears about 350 times in the spec, 270 in the architecture and 600 in the twelve designs, and in output that agents read (`3 replaced items also matched`, the `items` counts of `index` and `tags`), so it is a spec change with every design re-reviewed. What the owner's question does show is that the architecture never says what an item is where the type is declared. I would add one sentence there: "An item is one Markdown file of the brain, holding one piece of knowledge and its header; a document from outside becomes a `source` item, or several items drawn from it." Rename: never. Clarifying sentence: now.
