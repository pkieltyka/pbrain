# BM25 in v1 — @architect-2

**Ship `search --bm25` for an agent who wants a broader keyword search.** Both flags together request all three methods. Return the original exact list, then qualifying semantic results in their existing order, then qualifying BM25-only items. A caller without a model can request exact plus BM25. This preserves both established first pages, apart from normal footer/related-line budget effects. On a small page there may be room for only the exact hits. Page forward to reach the rest.

**Alternatives.** BM25 within exact classes cannot recover any of the 19 fully empty searches and can disturb the decision, age and standing order. Putting a BM25 tail ahead of semantic results spends the scarce first page on a weaker source of matches (5 right first versus 13 for bge in the pilot). Equal-weight fusion placed 11 right first and 16 in the top three versus 12 and 17 for the approved exact-plus-semantic rule. Allow a broad candidate to appear once only, under its strongest *tier*. Keep existing semantic text for a hit both approximate methods find. Mark only newly added rows `partial` (JSON `partial: true`); show their existing short passage as evidence. A mark explains provenance but cannot make an irrelevant item useful. [Prior comparison](./joint.md)

**Admission and ranking are separate decisions.** Compare these two starter gates on held-out tasks; freeze the chosen rule before evaluating it. Both reuse pbrain’s existing folded clause grammar, require every quoted phrase and explicit prefix to hold in one field, and collapse repeated clauses. For the first prototype, a “distinctive” clause occurs in at most half the eligible items. Require two matched distinctive clauses and at least half of all distinctive clauses, including those with zero occurrences. Do not discard an unknown identifier as a stop word. A one-clause query gains no partial hit. A two-distinctive-clause query must match both. A stricter alternative requires two matched clauses plus half of all inverse-document-frequency weight; it can be too severe on paraphrases. A single global raw BM25 score has no consistent meaning across different query lengths and brains.

In a scratch replay with the original 50 sections, ASCII words, no stop list and `k1=1.2, b=.75`, the distinctive-count gate placed the right section first on 4/20 and in the top three on 7/20; it returned something for 2/5 unrelated questions. The stricter weighted gate returned nothing for all five, but top-three retrieval fell to 2/20. Both are standalone rankings, without the exact tier, model, footer or output fitter. **These are engineering starting points, not release thresholds.** Include close negatives from the same component, a wrong version and a missing identifier. The five easy negatives cannot select a threshold.

**Implementation.** Score in Go over the live eligible items, sharing the tokenizer and clause matcher. Track each candidate’s length and query-clause frequencies, then compute document frequencies from this view and sort admitted items by positive-IDF BM25, ties by creation time, id, path. Sum fields without adding an untested title or heading weight. A measured 5,000-item fresh-process run must cover the 300 ms model-free path. FTS5 can score cheaply when a current token-compatible index exists, but the approved SQLite *vector* store alone supplies neither that index nor equivalent tokenisation, isolation or availability.

The first tier owns decision promotion and exact-only counts of hidden replacements, out-of-window matches and individual clauses. Apply scope, filters and current/replaced visibility before collecting BM25 statistics. In the partial tier under `--all`, current items precede replaced ones. Page and budget all deduplicated results together. A missing semantic index leaves exact plus requested BM25 available with the usual semantic notice. No new lookup hint. The accepted prefix skill line and fixture expansion still stand.
