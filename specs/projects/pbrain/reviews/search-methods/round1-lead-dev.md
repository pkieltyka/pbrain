# Search methods · @lead-dev

**Keep v1 strict lexical plus opt-in semantic.** Use qmd as a comparison in the evaluation fixture. Its strongest ideas for pbrain are structure-aware passages, cited locations, reuse of unchanged embeddings and measuring retrieval under actual output limits. The approved design already has those properties.

| Method | Gain, cost and decision |
|---|---|
| **BM25 and any-word matching** | BM25 orders candidates, while OR determines what qualifies. On 20 reworded, answerable questions, OR plus BM25 found the right section first 5 times and in the top three 11 times. Strict lexical returned 19 empty pages, while bge-small put 13 first and 17 in the top three. BM25 returned something for every question, and the test had no unanswerable questions. The prompts were whole sentences about one spec, so these results say little about short engineering queries, abstention or the incremental gain over hybrid. **Later, if it earns a place.** First compare OR ranked by distinct matched clauses with OR plus BM25. If it adds useful hits on the remaining misses, try it as an explicit keyword-broadening mode for empty searches or model-free use. Preserve filters and require meaningful evidence. Avoid automatic fallback and a third hybrid leg in v1. With a 2,000-character page, weak hits can crowd out the useful passage. [Retrieval probe](../../research/semantic-search-and-index/embedding-models-and-go-runtime/cpu-measurements.md#a-small-retrieval-check-on-pbrains-own-text) · [BGE spike](../../research/semantic-search-and-index/embedding-models-and-go-runtime/spike-bge-small-hugot.md#6-the-20-question-check) |
| **Rank fusion** | qmd's RRF combines incomparable rank lists cheaply, and agreement across methods can help ordering. It cannot make a poor candidate relevant. Full fusion could move a good literal result or the decision in force behind a plausible paraphrase. **Keep lexical-first in v1.** If useful semantic evidence repeatedly fails to fit on the first page, evaluate rank fusion on independently eligible candidates. Keep the absolute semantic cutoff and owner-approved ordering rules in the comparison. [qmd pipeline](../../research/semantic-search-and-index/qmd-and-go-index-engine/qmd-pipeline-and-tradeoffs.md#how-qmd-searches) |
| **Expansion and reranking** | Expansion can bridge project shorthand and prose. A cross-encoder can distinguish relevant uses of the same words. We have no measured gain from those two stages on engineering memory, while qmd's separate local models add substantial downloads, inference time and tuning. **Defer both beyond v1.** If poor ordering remains a proven problem, try expansion and reranking separately on the same cases, with the same local and latency constraints. [qmd costs](../../research/semantic-search-and-index/qmd-and-go-index-engine/qmd-pipeline-and-tradeoffs.md) |
| **Lexical refinements** | **V1:** keep exact phrases and prefixes, and give titles and tags their existing match classes. Exact identifiers, commands and error phrases benefit from those predictable rules. **Later, on specific misses:** proximity within an eligible match, a title/tag/heading weight experiment, and conservative typo or inflection suggestions. Automatic stemming and fuzzy matching risk rewriting identifiers and being language-dependent. Field weights can bury useful body evidence if copied from another corpus. Learn from the shape of qmd's weighting, then tune on pbrain's tasks. [qmd lexical implementation](../../research/semantic-search-and-index/qmd-and-go-index-engine/qmd-pipeline-and-tradeoffs.md#how-qmd-searches) |
| **Passages, context and graph** | **V1:** keep heading/paragraph boundaries, roughly 1,400 code points, deterministic title/heading context, stable IDs and real body offsets. Use `get --outline` and open the section that answers the question. qmd's roughly 900 tokens and 15% overlap would exceed bge-small's 512-token input and multiply vectors. Test overlap only on misses caused by a cut. Keep recorded relations and recency bands visible and bounded. Graph-driven rank boosts or recency overrides can amplify popular or recent material without proving it answers the question. **Later:** test those only on paired tasks that separate current guidance from historical or source evidence. [Chunking research](../../research/semantic-search-and-index/long-documents-and-pageindex/long-document-approaches.md) |

**Evaluate before adding knobs.** Compare the approved modes with the same query, view, character allowance and first page. Add short exact queries, rewordings, boundary-spanning questions and unanswerable ones. Measure expected IDs and useful spans, false hits, answer quality, total characters and calls, p95 latency, build cost and zero personal leakage. Use qmd's labeled-query approach. Keep pbrain's own access boundary and output envelope.

**Runtime and storage are separate decisions.** The approved baseline uses hugot/BGE and flat vectors. A move to goinfer or sqlite-vec changes timing, model-specific calibration and operational cost, while the advice about query eligibility and ranking stands. The scoring probe at 50,000 × 768 measured 39 ms in Go and 57–60 ms in sqlite-vec; choose on full workflow evidence if the owner wants to reopen storage.
